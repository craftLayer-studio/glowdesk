# GlowDesk pros, cons and risks

## Pros

| Pro | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Huge, fragmented market | Unorganised operators hold an estimated 70–80% of salon revenue (see 04). Few have software. |
| Predictable repeat cycle | Hair and grooming services repeat every 2–8 weeks, so rebooking nudges have a clear trigger. |
| WhatsApp already the default channel | Clients already message salons on WhatsApp. No behaviour change needed. |
| Visible ROI | Recovered revenue can be shown in rupees, which makes renewal easy. |
| Low build cost | 70%+ of features come from the CraftLayer core (QR, booking, CRM, messaging, reviews, payments). |
| Cheap to demo | A QR standee and a 2-minute demo on the owner's own phone. |
| Expansion revenue | WhatsApp credits, memberships module, extra outlets, SMS, printed QR kits. |

## Cons

| Con | Impact |
|---|---|
| Crowded market with cheap local players (Invoay from ₹799, DINGG ₹999–₹2,499, MioSalon, Salonist, Zylu) | Price pressure; we must differentiate on outcome, not features. |
| Low willingness to pay in Tier 3 | ARPU may sit near ₹800, making field sales expensive. |
| Staff resistance | Stylists may fear the owner tracking them; receptionists fall back to paper (DINGG itself reports this failure mode, see 04). |
| Meta controls WhatsApp pricing | Marketing template cost rose about 10% on 1 January 2026 (see 04). Margins on credits can shrink. |
| Seasonality | Wedding season and festivals spike; monsoon and exam months dip. |
| Many tiny outlets churn | Small salons open and close often. |

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owners sign up but never put QR on mirror | High | High | Founder or field rep installs QR standees on day one; activation call on day 3; free tier counts only after 30 clients captured. |
| Nudges feel like spam, clients block the number; Meta lowers quality rating | Medium | High | Separate marketing opt-in; cap 2 marketing messages per client per month; personalised copy with stylist name; monitor block rate; pause campaigns if quality drops. |
| DPDP non-compliance (consent, purpose, deletion) | Medium | High | Consent logged with timestamp and notice version; opt-out in every message; data deletion on request; GlowDesk acts as data processor for the salon (data fiduciary). Core obligations under DPDP Rules 2025 apply from about May 2027; build compliant from day one. |
| Before/after photos misused | Low | High | Per-photo consent, face blur option, private by default. |
| Competitor copies rebooking nudges | High | Medium | Speed, service and the recovered-revenue dashboard; win on onboarding and local support, not features. |
| Fresha or Booksy enters India aggressively with free tiers | Low–medium | High | Fresha already moved from free to paid subscriptions in 2025 (see 04). Lean on Hindi/regional language support, UPI, GST billing, local sales. |
| WhatsApp BSP outage or account ban | Low | High | Two BSP integrations behind an adapter; SMS fallback for critical utility messages. |
| Founder spends months building ERP features (payroll, accounting) | Medium | High | Hard scope rule: no feature unless 5 paying salons ask and it moves a metric. |
| CAC too high for ₹799 plan | Medium | High | Push annual prepaid; sell Growth tier by default; use referral and distributor channels (see 06). |

## Kill criteria

Decide at the dates below. If a criterion is hit, stop GlowDesk or pivot the wedge.

| Checkpoint | Kill or pivot if |
|---|---|
| Week 8 (MVP pilot, 5–10 salons) | Fewer than 3 pilot salons have QR live and 50+ clients captured |
| Week 12 | Fewer than 10 paying salons after 100+ in-person demos |
| Month 4 | Median recovered revenue below 2x the subscription fee across paying salons |
| Month 6 | Monthly logo churn above 8% for 2 consecutive months, or free-to-paid below 10% |
| Month 6 | Blended CAC payback above 9 months |
| Month 9 | Fewer than 60 paying outlets or MRR below ₹90,000 |

Pivot options before killing: (a) sell to salon distributors and product brands as a retention tool for their salon network, (b) narrow to men's barbershops only (high walk-in, queue value strongest), (c) fold features into another CraftLayer product.
