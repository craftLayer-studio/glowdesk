# GlowDesk problems and benefits

Figures marked (estimate) are reasoned assumptions to be replaced with pilot data. Sourced figures link to [04 market research](04-market-research.md).

## Owner

| Problem | GlowDesk solution | Benefit | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clients drift away silently. Owner cannot tell who stopped coming. | Due-list and service-cycle rebooking nudges on WhatsApp | More repeat visits; recovered revenue shown in rupees | Retention, revenue |
| No-shows on long services (colour, bridal, spa) waste 1–3 chair-hours | Reminders 24 h and 2 h before; optional deposit | Vendors report 40–45% fewer no-shows with WhatsApp reminders (vendor claims, see 04) | Revenue |
| Weekend crowd: clients argue about turn, some leave | QR walk-in queue with live position | Fewer walk-outs; clients wait at the tea shop instead of the sofa | Revenue |
| Client data lives in stylists' personal phones; senior stylist leaves and takes regulars | Client list owned by the salon, stylist sees only first name + history in staff app | Business keeps its clients when staff change | Retention |
| Commission disputes every month end | Rule-based commission, visible daily to each stylist | Fewer disputes, less owner time on maths | Activation |
| Cash leakage: no record of who billed what | Every bill tied to stylist and payment mode; daily close | Owner trusts the numbers without being at the counter | Activation |
| Few Google reviews, rank below chain salons | Review request after every visit, routes happy clients to Google | More reviews, better local search rank | Acquisition |
| Packages sold on paper cards get lost or disputed | Digital packages and memberships with balance on client's WhatsApp | More prepaid cash; fewer disputes | Revenue |
| Consumables (colour, wax, bleach) run out mid-day | Low-stock alert based on services done (P1) | No lost services; less emergency buying | Retention |

## Staff (stylists, receptionist)

| Problem | Solution | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Phone rings during service; receptionist juggles calls and counter | QR self-booking and queue | Fewer calls, calmer front desk |
| Stylist does not know what they earned until month end | Live commission in staff app | Motivation to upsell products and packages |
| Forgetting a regular's colour formula or allergy | Client notes visible at check-in | Better service, fewer complaints |
| Credit disputes between stylists over walk-ins | Queue assigns turn fairly or to chosen stylist | Less friction |

## End customer (client)

| Problem | Solution | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Waits 30–60 minutes on Sunday without knowing when | Live queue position on phone, "your turn in 5 minutes" message | Can step out, less frustration |
| Has to call to book; calls go unanswered | Book via link in WhatsApp or Instagram in 30 seconds | Convenience |
| Forgets when the last haircut was | Polite reminder when due | Looks good without planning |
| Unsure about prices | Menu with prices before booking | Trust |
| Cannot see stylist's work | Before/after gallery (with consent) | Confident choice |
| Spam fear | Clear opt-in, max 2 marketing messages a month, one-tap opt-out | Control over own data |

## The ROI story for the sales pitch (estimate)

A 5-chair salon with 400 active clients and ₹600 average bill:

- 400 clients get a nudge on their cycle in a month (most services are monthly or close to it).
- About 300 have marketing opt-in (75%, estimate).
- 15% book within 14 days because of the nudge rather than by habit (estimate; vendor claims of 25% higher repeat rate exist, see 04) = 45 visits.
- 45 × ₹600 = **₹27,000 recovered revenue per month** against ₹1,499 subscription plus about ₹400 WhatsApp credits.

Even at one-third of this, the salon earns about 4–5x its spend. The pitch is: "If GlowDesk does not show ₹5,000 recovered in 30 days, do not pay for month two."
